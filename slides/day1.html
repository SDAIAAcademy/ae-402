The architect's loop

Architecture is a loop from drivers to decisions, then back

1 · Driversgoals, constraints, concerns
→
2 · Scenariostargets with numbers
→
3 · Optionsthree, costed
→
4 · Decisionsrecorded, with triggers
→
5 · Reviewtested against scenarios

Views are drawn all along the loop. A review that finds a failed scenario sends you back to step 3, not to a slide tool.

This is the spine of the four days. Day 1 is steps 1 and 2 plus the views; Day 2 is options and decisions; Day 3 applies the loop to the shared platform; Day 4 is review and enforcement.

Ask: in your last project, which of these five steps happened only in someone's head?

What changes for AI systems

An AI system adds four attributes a classic design rarely names

Answer quality

Correctness varies by question type, language and model version.

Cost per request

Tokens make cost a function of each prompt, not of a server count.

Data in prompts

Every prompt is a data flow to wherever the model runs.

Human oversight

Some answers must go to a person, by design.

What goes wrong. These four are discovered after launch, by a complaint, a bill or a data protection officer.

Latency, availability and security still matter; they are covered by AE-403 and AE-308 in depth. This course makes these four first-class from the first hour, because each one changes where the system can run and what it costs.

The scenario

A scenario has six parts, and the last one carries a number

Sourcean engineering office
→
Stimulusasks any question
→
Environmenta normal working day
→
ArtefactMuyassir via the gateway
→
Responsea complete answer
→
Measurep95 ≤ 4,000 ms
Testable. The measure names a metric that is recorded, a statistic and a target.

The six-part form comes from the quality-attribute scenarios of the SEI's architecture methods, the same tradition as the review method of Day 4. Read the example aloud as one sentence.

Sources: SEI, The Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method.

Oversight is a number too

Human oversight becomes the share of cases that reach a person

applicantWill my extension be approved if I move the stairs?

Target 0.99. The pilot reaches 0.87: it fails.

From Lab 1.2. The concern of the inspectors (S7) and the disclosure concern (S14) both become measures here. The ratio is from the synthetic pilot. Ask: is 0.99 too strict? The inspectors own that target, not the architect.

Choose the statistic

For privacy, count the leaks: a mean hides the one that matters

1 of 400
pilot answers showed another applicant's file (request P0288)
  • Mean leak rate: 0.0025. It looks like success.
  • Sum of leaks with a target of zero: it fails, as it should.
  • One leak of a deed is a reportable incident, not a rate.

The statistic is part of the requirement. Means suit quality, percentiles suit latency, sums or maxima suit harms. P0288 is planted in the synthetic pilot; ask what in the design could cause it (a status read keyed on the wrong identifier).

03
National data classification

Four levels, set by the impact of unauthorised disclosure

LevelImpact of disclosureIn the Ministry
Top Secrethighnone in this case
Secretmediumtender documents, land disputes, state land surveys
Restrictedlowapplicant files, status records, conversation logs
Publicnonethe published building code, fee schedule, zoning maps

The four levels and the impact scale are in the Data Classification Policy issued by the National Data Management Office. The examples are the case's own inventory. Participants should know the levels already (it is a prerequisite); spend the time on what they decide.

Sources: NDMO Data Classification Policy.

Three principles that shape designs

Open by default, highest level wins, classify at creation

Open by default

Data is accessible unless its nature or sensitivity requires more protection.

Highest level

A dataset that combines levels takes the highest of them. A prompt is such a dataset.

Timely

Data is classified when created or received, so a new log needs a level on day one.

All three are principles of the NDMO policy, paraphrased. The second is the one architects trip on: a retrieval prompt that holds a public clause and an applicant's identity number is Restricted as a whole. The third explains Lab 1.3's blanks: the conversation log is new, so it has no level until someone gives it one.

Sources: NDMO Data Classification Policy.

Personal data outside the Kingdom

Transfer abroad is a regulated path, not a hosting preference

Adequacy

The competent authority publishes the countries and organisations that give an appropriate level of protection.

Safeguards

Otherwise, in set cases: standard contractual clauses, binding common rules or an accreditation certificate.

Onward transfer

The Law still applies when data moves on from the first recipient.

For the architecture. Each flow that could leave the Kingdom names its path, or does not leave.

Paraphrased from the Regulation on Personal Data Transfer outside the Kingdom, read for this course. Do not turn this slide into legal advice: the design states which flows carry personal data out and on what basis, and the data protection officer decides the basis. The Ministry in our case simply chose not to transfer Restricted data at all.

Sources: Regulation on Personal Data Transfer outside the Kingdom; Personal Data Protection Law.

The budget

The request costs half its budget and runs 290 ms over its time

All three numbers from the solution run of Lab 1.4 (the third is the stretch). Removing the reranker for status questions fits the time budget, and it reopens Q1: answer quality must be re-tested on those questions. That trade-off is a decision record on Day 2.

Reading a budget

A sum of p95s is an upper bound; check it against one measurement

  • Slow tails of different hops rarely meet in one request, so the sum overstates.
  • Retries and queues add time no single hop shows, so the sum can also understate.
  • The pilot measured 4,866 ms end to end: here the bound was not pessimistic.
What goes wrong. A budget built from p50s. It fits on paper, and half the users wait longer.

The gap between 4,290 and 4,866 comes from long-context retries planted in the pilot (every 23rd request). The lesson: budget per hop to find where to cut, measure end to end to know whether you are done.

Checkpoint

Which change can bring Muyassir under 4,000 ms?

The sum of p95s is 4,290 ms: model 2,900, reranker 650, status read 400, the rest 340. Which single change most likely brings the p95 under 4,000 ms without a new model?

Write the conversation log faster
Tune the index for a faster median query
Add a second gateway check before the model
Skip reranking for status questions and send fewer clauses
Why. The reranker's 650 ms and the context size are the levers in the architect's hands, and the sum drops to 3,640 ms; answer quality must then be re-tested.

Answer: D. Keep the follow-up for Day 2: dropping a hop is a decision with a bad consequence (answer quality on some questions), so it gets a record with a review trigger.
