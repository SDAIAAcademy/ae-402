The gateway

One door for every model call, with five duties

Credentials

A key per team; provider keys never leave the gateway.

Quotas and budgets

Per key: requests a minute, spend.

Fallback

Another admitted model when one fails, under the same policies.

Tool mediation

Only the tools a team is allowed reach the model.

Audit

Who, which model, which level, which decision.

And now: policy

Classification decides where a request may go.

Products in this slot: LiteLLM's proxy offers virtual keys with budgets and rate limits per key, fallbacks, and guardrails that run before or after a call; agentgateway proxies model, agent and MCP traffic. Lab 3.2, step 4

The level of a request is the highest it shows, declares or defaults to

def classification_policy(ctx):
    levels = [G.detect_level(ctx["text"])]
    levels.append(ctx["request"].get("classification")
                  or ctx["key_info"]["max_level"])   # fail closed
    level = A.highest(levels)
    ctx["level"] = level
    hosting = ctx["entry"]["hosting"]
    if not A.allowed(level, hosting):
        return 403, f"{level} data may not go to a model hosted at '{hosting}'"

Three sources of the level: what the request declares, what the content shows, and, when it declares nothing, the team's highest level. Undeclared from a Restricted team means Restricted. That default is a decision; it makes teams declare Public explicitly when they mean it.

03
Tenancy

Tenancy is a choice of what teams share and what each gets alone

ModelTeams shareEach team getsFits
Pooledkey, budget, capacitynothinga pilot
Per-team controlscapacity, under capsa key, a budget, a capped sharePublic and Restricted teams

Most platforms mix them: per-team controls for most teams, dedicated deployments where data or load demands it. The lab compares the first two; the third is a cost question the options paper of a Secret team answers.

One hour, three incidents

Three incidents test three different controls

Leaked key

The planning team's key calls the external model 400 times a minute for 15 minutes.

Noisy neighbour

The contact team's batch floods the Ministry-hosted model with 300 extra requests a minute.

Runaway agent

An inspections agent loops for 40 minutes at six times the tokens per call.

The replay is deterministic (asas_tenancy.py). The Ministry-hosted model serves 120 requests a minute; a quiet hour of paid models costs SAR 99.

Under the pooled model, the leak and the runaway agent exhaust the one shared budget and cut every team off. Per team: the leaked key is revoked at minute 30 when its rate exceeds five times normal; the runaway agent hits its own budget and spike rule.

Which control for which incident

Each incident needs its own control; the tenancy model is the set

ControlContainsDoes nothing for
A cap on each team's share of capacitynoisy neighbourspend
A budget per keyrunaway agentcapacity
A key per team, revoked on a spikeleaked keya slow, legitimate overload

The stretch finds the largest safe cap: 0.5 of capacity holds for this hour's noisy neighbour. A smaller cap is safer and slows the culprit's legitimate batch more; that trade-off belongs in a decision record.

The principle

One team's incident should stay that team's incident

5 minutes
without paid models for the planning team, until its new key
  • Revocation costs the team whose key leaked; nobody else.
  • Keys are scoped to the models of the team's level: a leaked Public key reaches no Restricted model.
  • Rotation is routine, so revoking is not a crisis.

Under the pooled model the only way to stop the leak is to revoke the key all nine teams use: an outage for everyone. Most teams then do not revoke, and the budget burns instead.

Checkpoint

Which control stops this from harming other teams?

The contact team's nightly batch moves to working hours and asks the Ministry-hosted model for more than twice its capacity. Budgets are untouched; the other teams' answers slow down. Which control fixes it?

A lower budget on the contact team's key
Revoke the contact team's key
A cap on any team's share of the Ministry-hosted model
Fall back to a cloud model when the queue is long
Why. The harm is shared capacity; only a cap on each team's share keeps the others served.

Answer: C. D is worth a minute: call transcripts are Restricted, so a cloud region in the Kingdom would be allowed by the rule; the external model would not. Placement still limits the fallback.

04
Cost attribution

Show teams their costs before anyone is charged

Showback

Each team sees what its use cost, at any granularity. Nothing moves between budgets.

Chargeback

The costs are sent to the teams' budgets. Allocation keys now move money.

Order. Run showback until the teams stop disputing the allocation keys; then decide about chargeback.

The FinOps Framework distinguishes the two: chargeback is the formal sending of costs to accounting budgets, showback is reporting without it. Nisreen Al-Mehaimeed asked for showback first; the lab shows why the keys need arguing before money moves.

Sources: FinOps Framework, invoicing and chargeback.

Untagged spend

Untagged spend comes in two kinds, and only one has an owner

Blank tag, known key

116 rows, SAR 13,950: the key's owner is known, so the gateway attributes them.

Orphan key

k-legacy-01: SAR 558 over six months, chat on a cloud model, owned by nobody.

The fix is a rule, not a report. No key without an owner, and a key without one is revoked.

The orphan's spend is 0.14% of the six months, small enough to be ignored, which is why it matters: nobody would notice it grow, and it can reach a model that receives Restricted data.
