Review of Day 3

Which control contains a leaked key to one team?

Asas moves from one shared key to per-team keys. The planning team's key leaks and calls the external model 400 times a minute. What keeps the other eight teams served?

Revoking that team's key when its rate spikes, under per-team budgets
A cap on each team's share of the Ministry-hosted model
A larger shared budget for the hour
A showback that names the planning team
Why. A per-team key can be revoked without touching anyone else, and a per-team budget bounds the spend until it is.

Answer: A. In Lab 3.3 every bystander kept 100% of paid service under the per-team model; under the shared one the worst lost 38%.

01
Golden paths

Make the compliant way the easiest way to start

A working start

A service a team generates in minutes, not a document it reads.

Gates on by default

Evaluation, classification checks and tracing come with it.

Optional, and visible

A team may leave the path; the platform sees who did and why.

What goes wrong. A golden path that is harder than doing it yourself. Teams route around it, and the gates go with it.

The CNCF platforms paper calls these templates and documentation for a paved road. Backstage software templates and Copier fill the same slot: Backstage when there is a developer portal, Copier when a command line and a repository are enough. Asas has no portal, so the lab uses Copier.

Sources: CNCF Platforms White Paper; Backstage software templates; Copier documentation.

The waste team in Lab 4.1. A gate that can be switched off silently is a suggestion; one that needs a reason and appears in a register is a standard with a visible exit. Session 4 is the same idea for a bigger exception.

Template plus update

A template copied once stops at the day it was copied

Copied

Each service drifts; a fix to the gates reaches nobody who started before it.

Generated and updated

The service records its answers; copier update brings later template changes, and conflicts show as a diff.

The lab's template writes Copier's answers file into every service for this reason. Updating needs the template under version control with tagged releases; the lab does not run an update, to keep within 25 minutes.

Sources: Copier documentation.

Fitness functions

Standard"Restricted data never leaves the Kingdom"
→

Thoughtworks' radar describes an architectural fitness function as an objective assessment of an architectural characteristic, run continuously. The design file is the container and data-flow views of Day 1 in a form a program can read; Structurizr models can be exported the same way.

Sources: Thoughtworks Technology Radar, architectural fitness function.

The placement check

Follow every fallback: it is where the data goes when things fail

F2kashif → vision · Restricted
visionksa-vision-x · ksa_cloud · allowed
fallbackvision_ext · ext-vision-q · external · not allowed
FAILkashif: flow F2 sends Restricted data to vision_ext (fallback of vision), hosted external

The container view looks compliant: the primary model is in the Kingdom. The same lesson as Lab 3.2's stretch, now at design time: a fallback must pass the same rule as the first choice.

Same idea, other tools

The language of the check matters less than where it runs

conftest runs Rego policies against structured files; it was not run in this course's build because it is a binary download, and the Python checks do the same job here. Choose the language the platform team will maintain.

Sources: Conftest; Open Policy Agent documentation.

What a check cannot see

A check is only as true as the design file

  • A file that says "ksa_cloud" for a service deployed abroad passes.
  • An evaluation set in the wrong language passes check 1.
  • A design that meets every rule may still miss its scenarios.

So

Checks catch what can be read from the file on every commit; a review asks whether the file and the design are right, once per design.

The stretch adds a third check, for an Arabic evaluation set; Kashif fails it. Bridge to Session 3: the review finds what no check could.

Session 3

Structured architecture review

  • Prioritise scenarios in a utility tree
  • Tell risks, sensitivity points, trade-off points and non-compliance apart
  • Write a review report with a verdict a team can act on
03
A structured review

Review against prioritised scenarios, not against a checklist

Driversthe business goals and constraints
→
Utility treescenarios by importance and difficulty
→
Analysisthe approaches against the leading scenarios
→
Findingsrisks, non-risks, sensitivity and trade-off points
→
Risk themestied to the drivers

The Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method from the SEI runs over days with stakeholders; the lab runs its core in 25 minutes on one design. The Ministry adds one tag of its own, non-compliance, for a finding that breaks a written standard.

Sources: The Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method (SEI).

Kashif's utility tree

Three scenarios lead: Arabic quality, privacy and security

ScenarioAttributeImportanceDifficulty
Q1 · Arabic shop signs read correctly in 90% of the Ministry's setanswer qualityHH
P1 · no photo with faces or plates processed outside the KingdomprivacyHM
S1 · a leaked key contained to one client within 30 minutessecurityHM
O1 · every filed report approved by its inspectoroversightHL
L1 · a draft on the tablet within 5 s at p95 on 4GlatencyMH

Five of eight scenarios; cost, availability and operability follow.

Importance is the business's; difficulty is the architect's estimate. Oversight is important and easy here, so it does not lead the review, which is not the same as not mattering.

What a finding is

Five tags, each telling the team something different to do

TagMeansKashif example
Non-compliancebreaks a written standardR02 · photos fall back to an external model
Riskmay keep a scenario from being metR03 · evaluation in English only
Sensitivity pointone parameter a scenario depends onR04 · the vision call takes 3,800 of 5,000 ms
Trade-off pointone decision, two attributes in opposite directionsR05 · compressing photos: faster, cheaper, worse at small Arabic text
Non-riska decision that meets a scenarioR06 · the inspector approves every report

Non-risks belong in the report: they tell the team what not to change. The lab's self-check compares the participant's tags with the board chair's and prints the chair's reason for each disagreement.

Two findings about Arabic

A checklist finds the broken rule; a reviewer finds the empty gate

R01 · non-compliance

The vision model was not admitted: no Arabic evaluation evidence. Any checklist catches it.

R03 · risk

An evaluation gate exists and blocks. It runs on English, and the signs are Arabic. Only someone who read Q1 sees it.

Both fall under the first risk theme: Arabic answer quality is unproven. The stretch of Lab 4.3 asks which findings could become fitness checks next time: R03 can, now that a reviewer has named it.

Not approved, with the four themes that would change the verdict

2 · 3 · 2 · 2 · 3

Checkpoint

How should the board tag this observation?

"A 10% sample review of approved reports is the only check that inspectors' approvals are real." The oversight scenario requires every filed report to carry an approval.

Non-compliance
A sensitivity point: whether approvals mean anything rests on that one rate
A non-risk, since every report is approved
A trade-off point
Why. A sensitivity point is one parameter a scenario depends on; lower the sample rate and rubber-stamping goes unseen.

Session 4

Deviations and sponsors

  • Put numbers on what a deviation breaks and what refusing it costs
  • Decide a deviation with verifiable conditions, an expiry and reopen triggers
  • Explain a trade-off on one page for a sponsor
04
Deviations

A deviation is a decision record with an expiry

Conditions

Each with a number a reviewer can check.

Residual risk

In numbers, and who accepts it.

Expiry

A date, and the events that reopen it sooner.

What goes wrong. A deviation without an expiry becomes the new standard by default, with nobody having decided that.

Same template as Lab 2.4's decision records, plus three fields. The board's job is not to say no; it is to make the exception bounded and visible.

Measured in Lab 4.4

SAR 96,000
a month of inspector time if refused: 800 hours

From 400 labelled synthetic photos: 30% show faces, plates or documents; the on-device detector let 2 of the 121 Restricted ones through. Two misses is a thin estimate, so the conditions ask for a measurement on 1,000 photos within 30 days.

Three answers

Approve with conditions, until the evidence in the Kingdom arrives

AnswerGivesCosts
Refusethe rule kept wholeSAR 96,000 a month of inspector time, until February 2027 at least
Approve as askeddrafts for every photo5,445 Restricted photos a month abroad, for twelve months

The expiry is a month after ksa-vision-x's evidence is expected. Reopen triggers: that evidence at or above the catalogue threshold, a measured miss rate above 3%, or any flagged photo in the external model's log. Whether 90 a month is acceptable is the data protection officer's call, not the architect's.

One page for a sponsor

The decision first, the trade-off in numbers, and a date it ends

  • The recommendation in the first sentence.
  • What it gives and what it risks, in hours, riyals and photos.
  • What she is asked to accept, by name.
  • When and how it ends.

Left out on purpose

Gateway, fallback, p95, fitness check. The lab's self-check fails the page if any of them appear, or if it runs past 250 words.

The solution's page is 205 words. Dr. Afrah decides on outcomes; the mechanism is in the decision record for whoever needs it. Ask the room to read the solution page aloud in under a minute.

Paths, not documents

Checks and reviews

Bounded exceptions

A deviation has conditions, a residual someone accepts and a date it ends.
